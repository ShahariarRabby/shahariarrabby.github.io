Research Vision

My research develops constraint-driven machine learning methods that bridge algorithmic innovation and deployable systems. I focus on thin-structure and minority-class segmentation in medical imaging and structural monitoring, with emphasis on multi-institutional validation, topology and connectivity preservation, and real-world feasibility under memory and latency budgets.

Research Program

Thin-Structure Segmentation · Minority-Class Segmentation · Deployable AI (MLOps)

My work sits at the intersection of representation learning for thin, elongated, and minority-class structures and systems that are deployed and validated in the field — clinical and industrial, not just benchmarked.

The current thread began with a clinical question — can a lightweight model detect COPD from a single inspiratory chest CT, reliably across institutions? — and has driven a sequence of methods (COPDxNet, STRIPE, THREAD, dCrack) that progressively isolate what makes thin-structure segmentation hard: topology, connectivity, and class imbalance, under tight memory and latency budgets.

A five-year research plan. As a Research Scientist at UAB, this line is now active: I am building 3D pulmonary fissure segmentation and an algorithmic fissure-integrity score that quantifies the completeness and connectivity of fissures across the lung. The integrity score is the foundation of the plan; over the next several years the program extends from it in three directions — deeper into the clinical question, to smaller structures, and across domains:

  1. Toward a clinical biomarker. Once the integrity score is established, pair it with longitudinal CT to test whether emphysema progresses differently near incomplete or damaged fissures — building on the voxel-level progression studies already funded under the NIH R01, combined with my fissure expertise — and correlate the score's measures of missing surface, gaps, fragmentation, and completeness with clinical variables (FEV1, GOLD severity, emphysema burden, air trapping), testing whether fissure integrity itself is a useful COPD measurement.
  2. To smaller structures. Generalize the thin-structure methods to finer, smaller targets in medical imaging — for example, mucus plugs in the airways — where the same topology- and connectivity-preservation principles apply at a different scale.
  3. Across domains. Extend the topology-preserving methods demonstrated on infrastructure cracks (dCrack) toward road and structural safety in collaboration with civil engineers, using the cross-domain transfer already shown (crack → retinal vessels) as evidence the methods generalize.

Foundations. Before the clinical thread, I built a substantial body of work in low-resource language processing and document understanding — Bangla OCR, handwriting, and sign-language datasets (Ekush, ISHARA-LIPI, Sankhya). That line trained the skills this program relies on: constructing large, carefully annotated datasets, and designing models that stay accurate on rare, minority-class inputs. Several of these datasets are now used by others, and the document-understanding methods feed directly into the vision work above.

Rabby, Chaudhary, Saha, Nakhmani, Zhang, Barr, Reinhardt, Bhatt, Bodduluri. Light Convolutional Neural Network for Detecting COPD: A Multicenter Model Development and External Validation

A lightweight 3D CNN (10.6M parameters) for population-scale COPD detection from inspiratory chest CT. Trained on COPDGene (21 centers, 13K+ CT scans); AUC 0.92 internally, with external validation on SPIROMICS (AUC 0.92) and NLST (AUC 0.82).

Rabby, Zhang, Nakhmani, Bodduluri, Bhatt. STRIPE: Directional Feature Extraction for Pulmonary Fissure Segmentation In preparation

Manuscript in preparation · First author

A directional encoder–decoder (AFM + DDA; LCNet-100 encoder; UNet++-like decoder) for pulmonary fissure segmentation. Systematic ablation shows encoder-level directional modeling preserves thin-structure continuity: Dice 0.873, HD95 0.72 mm, 5.15M parameters, evaluated on 50 COPDGene CT scans (5-fold CV).

Thin-Structure & Topology Segmentation

2026

Rabby, Zhang. THREAD: Efficient Topology-Preserving Network for Thin Structure Segmentation In preparation

Manuscript in preparation · First author

Topology-preserving architecture with a Directional Attention Bridge, Sobel-initialized Topology-Preserving Gate, and Cascaded Directional Refinement. On Crack500: F1 0.7940, clDice 0.8231, 48.6% fewer FLOPs; 92 FPS on GPU and 29.6 FPS on a Raspberry Pi 5 (~19× faster than SSMs). Cross-domain transfer: Dice 0.866 on pulmonary fissures without retraining.

Rabby, Zhang. dCrack: Enhancing Fine-Grained Crack Segmentation with Edge-Guided Attention ✦ dCrack61K

IEEE International Conference on Information Reuse and Integration (IRI), 2025 · First author

Edge-Guided Attention for minority-class crack segmentation, plus the dCrack61K benchmark (61,943 images, 36 sources, 2,000+ corrected annotations). Cross-domain transfer: F1 0.8251 on CHASE_DB1 retinal vessels without vessel-specific training.
